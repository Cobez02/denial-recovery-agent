# Plumbline Denial Recovery Pilot: Overview

Plumbline is denial-recovery software developed and owned by Betz Technology Providers (BTP).

## What Plumbline does

It reads the payment files your clearinghouse already receives (835 remittances) and a report of your
denied claims. For each denial it identifies why the payer denied it, proposes the fix (a corrected claim,
an appeal, or a question for your team), drafts the appeal letter using only the claim's facts and quotes
from your notes, and lists the work by deadline and dollar value. When the payer pays or denies again, the
next payment file closes the loop, and the dashboard shows what was recovered. A decision the payer sends
only by letter, such as an upheld Medicare redetermination, does not appear in a payment file; your
reviewer closes that item and records the result. For Medicare Part B, a correction is filed as a
reopening, or as a new claim when Medicare returned the claim as unprocessable; Plumbline's corrected
claim lists the change, and your biller files it the way the payer requires.

## What it does not do

- It never submits anything. Your team approves every item and submits it through your own system.
  Plumbline has no connection to any payer or clearinghouse.
- It never changes a code or adds a modifier on its own. A coding change cannot be approved until your
  reviewer attaches a quote from the note, and your coder or provider confirms that the quote supports
  each change.
- It does not contact patients, give coding or legal advice, or promise any amount of money back.

## How your patients' data is protected

- A business associate agreement is signed before any data is shared.
- The data is kept on one encrypted computer, in an account used only for this pilot, with no cloud
  synchronization, and on that computer's encrypted backup drive. Files move only through an encrypted file
  share or SFTP service, never by e-mail.
- Each of your reviewers has an individual login with a password and an authenticator app, and every
  approval, edit and export is recorded in an audit log.
- Plumbline starts out working entirely offline. An AI model is used only after a separate business
  associate agreement with the model provider is in place, with processing in the United States. Before
  anything is sent, Plumbline replaces the names, member identifiers, account and claim numbers, addresses,
  dates of birth and claim, remittance, note and letter dates it holds with placeholders, masks Social
  Security numbers, e-mail addresses and phone numbers, and blocks a request in which it still finds one of
  these.
  Other dates, names of other people and authorization or payment reference numbers can still be sent.
  Consumer AI tools never see your data.
- When the pilot ends, you receive a copy of your records in Plumbline (patients, claims, notes, letters,
  work items, proposals, review actions and outcomes); everything else, including remittance details and
  the audit log, is destroyed, and you receive a signed certificate of return and destruction.

## What we ask of your team

| When | What | Time |
|---|---|---|
| Before the start | Review and sign the business associate agreement and the pilot agreement (owner or an officer); a one-time data pull by your biller; payer deadlines from your contracts; account setup for each reviewer | A few hours in total |
| Weeks 1 to 3: shadow phase | Plumbline works denials you have already handled, and nothing is sent anywhere. In a weekly review, your biller's decisions are compared with Plumbline's | 30 minutes a week, plus your biller's notes on each compared item |
| Weeks 4 to 12: live phase | A weekly upload of new 835 files and denials; your reviewers approve, edit or reject each item at BTP's workstation, in a session BTP opens and attends; you submit what you approve | 30-minute weekly review; a target of under 5 minutes of review per item |
| End | Confirm receipt of your data copy | A few minutes |

Moving from the shadow phase to the live phase requires your written approval, after Plumbline has
matched your biller's decisions closely enough for two weeks in a row.

## Fees

The pilot agreement offers two options, selected on its cover page:

- **No fee.**
- **Success fee:** a percentage of the money actually recovered on corrected claims and appeals your team
  submitted from Plumbline's drafts, net of what the payer had already paid, applied to backlog claims only
  or to all pilot claims as selected, and capped at the amount on the cover page. Nothing is owed for the
  shadow phase, for items you reject or do not submit, if nothing is recovered, or on a recovery from a
  coding change later found unsupported by your notes.

BTP never keeps a fee on money a payer takes back: report the take-back within 12 months after the payment
and the fee is credited or refunded. After the pilot, recoveries and take-backs come from your payment
posting, reported by work item number; BTP keeps none of your data for this.

Either side can end the pilot with 30 days' notice.
