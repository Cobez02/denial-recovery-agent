## 2. Technology Errors and Omissions (Professional Liability)

1. Does the policy cover claims that the software or service caused the clinic a financial loss (a missed
   appeal deadline, an incorrect classification, an incorrect draft), including defense costs?
2. Does it exclude liability assumed under contract, such as reimbursing notification costs for breaches
   caused by the business or its subcontractor (business associate agreement, Section 2.3(e))? Can that
   exclusion be narrowed?
3. Does it exclude or limit claims arising from output generated by artificial intelligence or from
   algorithmic errors?
4. Is it written on a claims-made or occurrence basis? What is the retroactive date? What does an extended
   reporting period cost, and how long can it run?
5. Are defense costs inside or outside the limit? Who selects defense counsel? Is there a consent-to-settle
   clause?
6. Is it combined with the cyber policy, and if so, do the two share a single limit?
7. Are any of these excluded: fee disputes or the return of fees; billing and coding services; regulatory
   billing proceedings (payer audits, overpayment demands, False Claims Act allegations) over corrected
   claims and appeals that the business drafts and the clinic submits?

## 3. Cyber Liability

**First-party coverage** typically includes: legal counsel to determine notification and regulatory
obligations; recovery and replacement of lost or stolen data; notification of affected individuals and
call-center services; lost income from business interruption; crisis management and public relations;
cyber extortion and fraud; forensic investigation; and fees, fines and penalties related to the incident.

**Third-party coverage** typically includes: payments to individuals affected by a breach; claims and
settlement expenses; litigation costs and the costs of responding to regulatory inquiries; other
settlements, damages and judgments; accounting costs; and media liability (defamation, copyright or
trademark).

Confirm that the policy covers:

1. data breaches, including theft of personal information;
2. attacks on the business's own computer and network, such as ransomware, even when no data is taken;
3. attacks on data held by vendors and other third parties (here, the model provider and the file-transfer
   service);
4. attacks anywhere in the world, not only in the United States;
5. terrorist acts;
6. a duty to defend in lawsuits and regulatory investigations;
7. coverage in excess of other applicable insurance; and
8. a breach hotline available at all hours, every day.

Also ask:

9. What do the war and state-backed cyber-attack exclusions say, how is an attack attributed to a state,
   and would ransomware attributed to a state actor be excluded?
10. Is the cyber policy written on a claims-made or occurrence basis? What is the retroactive date, and what
    does an extended reporting period cost?

## 4. Questions Specific to a Business Associate

1. Does the policy cover protected health information the business holds for others (patient data in the
   business's care, custody or control)?
2. Does it pay notification, call-center and credit-monitoring costs when the clinic sends the notices and
   the business associate agreement requires the business to reimburse them?
3. Does it cover HIPAA regulatory proceedings (HHS Office for Civil Rights) and state attorney general
   actions, including penalties where insurable by law?
4. Does it cover an incident that begins at a subcontractor, such as the model provider?
5. Does it cover a home office and a home computer? (Homeowners and renters policies usually exclude
   business data and equipment.)
6. Are there exclusions for unencrypted devices or for failure to maintain the security controls described
   in the application? Which controls are conditions of coverage?
7. Are claims brought by a related party excluded, such as claims by an insured's family members or by
   businesses they own or operate? Claims by patients and regulators are not affected by such a
   relationship.
8. Will the insurer's breach counsel, forensic and notification vendors sign business associate agreements
   before they access protected health information?
9. How soon must an incident be reported to the insurer, and must the insurer approve vendors before they
   are engaged?
10. Is bodily injury or property damage to clinic staff visiting the home office for review sessions and
    account setup covered (a general liability policy or a home-business endorsement)?
